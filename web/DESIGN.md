# The frontend's visual direction and the path view

## The direction: a figure of a methods paper that can be used

idtrack answers a precise question (what is this identifier at that release?) for people who check evidence. The page is built as a measuring instrument and as a journal's figure rather than a dashboard: calm, exact, dense with meaning, generous with space; nothing that does not inform.

- **A strict grid and hairlines.** One content width (`--content`, 1280 px) on which every panel, figure, panel letter, rule and caption stands; a 4 px spacing scale (`--space-1` … `--space-9`); panels separated by 1 px rules (`--line`) rather than shadows. Controls are labelled like the panel of an instrument: a small label above the control, and the control's explanation always visible below it (never on hover alone).
- **Three faces, three jobs.** Inter (`--font-sans`) for the interface and the prose; Source Serif 4 (`--font-display`) for the page's title and the sections' headings; JetBrains Mono (`--font-mono`) for every identifier, release, score, digest and count, with slashed zeros and tabular figures, so that `ENSSSCG00000040723.1` reads as data and columns of numbers align. All three are served with the page (no font service); the Latin files of the three upright faces are preloaded by `index.html`. While a face loads, its fallback takes its place to within a few percent (`app.css`, "Faces": a local face scaled by `size-adjust` to the served face's advance, with its ascent and descent): the title in Source Serif 4 measures 695.6 px served and 704.6 px in Nimbus Roman. The fit of identifiers to the text's x-height (`--mono-fit`) applies only once JetBrains Mono has loaded (`main.tsx` sets `fonts-ready` on the root), and the monospaced fallbacks are scaled to the advance that fit gives, so an identifier takes the same width before and after the swap (an example identifier measures 105 px in both).
- **One ink scale, colour only for meaning.** Text, rules and controls use a neutral ink scale on a warm paper (light, the default) or a deep slate (dark). Hue appears only where it carries meaning: one hue per family of step, the same in every figure, its legend, its note and the path's reading; and the states' inks (`--state-ok`, `--state-warn`, `--state-danger`) for results and the server. Primary actions are ink. No gradients for their own sake.
- **Motion only where it explains**, 150 to 400 ms, and none under `prefers-reduced-motion`: a figure revealed once from left to right as time runs on its axis (the journeys and the path up close, 640 ms; the path view, 650 ms), a row isolated, the loading shimmer, the path view's zoom.
- **Both themes are designed, not inverted.** The dark theme has its own steps of the same hues and inks, checked separately. Light is the default; dark or the system's scheme can be chosen (kept in the browser), and the browser's own chrome (`theme-color`) takes the paper of the theme shown.

### Type

One scale of nine steps, in `src/styles/tokens.css`; nothing is set outside it, in any stylesheet or figure: no em-derived size, no fluid size, no pixel size of its own.

| Token | Size | Used for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `--text-2xs` | 11 px | the floor: figures' text, eyebrows, column heads, labels of values, small print in figures |
| `--text-xs` | 12 px | notes under values, legends, a figure's note, hints of a control |
| `--text-sm` | 13.5 px | tables, captions, the navigation, buttons |
| `--text-md` | 15 px | body text |
| `--text-lg` | 17 px | panel titles, ledes, a panel's letter |
| `--text-xl` | 21 px | the answer's identifier and headline below 900 px |
| `--text-2xl` | 28 px | a section's heading; the page's title below 720 px |
| `--text-3xl` | 40 px | the page's title (720 px and wider); the front page's display line from 760 to 1180 px |
| `--text-4xl` | 48 px | the front page's display line (wider than 1180 px) |

- **One heading system on every page, in three tiers** (`app.css`, "Type", and the tokens `--heading-title`, `--heading-section`, `--heading-panel`): the page's title (h1, the serif, 40 px; 28 px below 720 px); a section of the page (the serif at weight medium, 28 px; 21 px below 720 px): every section and lettered panel of every page (the answer's a to e, the home's a to d, Explore's and an organism's panels, Method's stages and sections, Names' two forms, the batch's result, the api page's sections, the load panel's title), named once by their selectors in `app.css`, each with its subtitle beside it and a rule under it as the api page sets them; a panel's title inside a section (the sans at weight strong, 17 px: the answer's sub-panels, the api page's cards, the load panel's parts). Below them the label (`.eyebrow`, `.label`). A page's stylesheet places its headings and never sets their face, size or weight (`tests/typescale.test.ts`); the end-to-end tests measure every page at 1440 and 390 px (`e2e/system.spec.ts`).
- **Three weights** for every face: `--weight-regular` 400 (text), `--weight-medium` 520 (a control, a label, the serif headings), `--weight-strong` 640 (a panel's title, a status, an answer, `strong`). No stylesheet sets a weight of its own (`tests/typescale.test.ts`).
- **One page head** (`.page-head`): the eyebrow (the section of the site: Convert, Convert a list, Names, Explore, Method, API) over the title, and the lede of at most 62 characters a line; stacked, and from 1180 px the lede beside them at the right from the same top line (so that the Convert page's form and figure keep the first screen). What a page adds (a scope note, the choice of organism) follows under them across the width. Every page, the page of an unknown address too, has it, but the Convert page: the hero is its own composition of the same parts, and over an answer the page's name stands alone above the form.
- **The page's title (h1) is the largest text of every page.** A section's heading is at most `--text-2xl`. The front page's title is its display line, a step larger (`--text-4xl` wider than 1180 px). Over an answer the page's title is its name in the eyebrow's type ("Convert"): the answer is the screen's subject, and the page has no intro (the Convert page, below).
- **Roles, each set once** (`src/styles/app.css`, "Type"): the eyebrow and the label (`.eyebrow`, `.label`: the monospaced face at `--text-2xs`, uppercase, tracked `--track-eyebrow` or `--track-label`; code inside a label keeps its case, since a path of the api is case-sensitive), small print (`.small`), the caption (`.fig-caption`, below), data (`.mono`, `code`).
- **An identifier inside running text keeps the text's size** and is fitted to its x-height (`font-size-adjust: var(--mono-fit)`), so the size stays on the scale and the line reads as one; a monospaced element whose size is set (a label, a value of a table) is not fitted.
- **Line lengths.** Prose and ledes `--measure` (68 characters); captions and legends `--measure-caption` (about 75 characters a line); under a figure as wide as the content, the caption is set in two columns of that measure (`.fig-caption--wide`).
- **Words break only where they may.** Every text wraps between words; a word is cut only when it alone is longer than its line, which only an identifier or what a visitor typed can be (`.mono`, and the boxes that echo input: `.empty`, `.notice`, `.page-head`, captions, notes, hints).

### Colour

| Token | Light | Dark | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `--hue-history` | `#2f5fd0` | `#5b8def` | Ensembl's id history: a new version, a replacement, a re-mapping of a gene to itself |
| `--hue-event` | `#c34a00` | `#e0702a` | Ensembl's id history: a split, a merge, or both |
| `--hue-external` | `#00805d` | `#1f9e78` | A crossing through an external database: an identifier read as a gene, a bridge through a shared id, the id in the target database |
| `--hue-handoff` | `#c0399a` | `#d45cb8` | A history step between genome assemblies |
| `--ink-1` … `--ink-3` | `#0e1116`, `#3b424c`, `#484e58` | `#eceef1`, `#b9bec6`, `#a5abb4` | Text, marks, rules; `--ink-3` reaches 7:1 on the paper, the surfaces and the bands in both themes (light: 8.0, 8.4, 7.5 and 7.3) |
| `--line`, `--line-strong` | `#e3e3de`, `#c3c5c9` | `#262b32`, `#434a54` | Hairlines and decorative rules |
| `--line-control` | `#80868f` | `#737b87` | The boundary of a control (an input, a select, the theme switch, a switch's track when off): 3:1 or more against what surrounds it |

The four hues were chosen with a computed check, not by eye, and `tests/palette.test.ts` keeps them so: every pair stays apart under simulated protanopia and deuteranopia (Machado, Oliveira and Fernandes 2009, full severity; OKLab ΔE ≥ 8, the worst pair 8.2 in light and 8.3 in dark) and in full colour (ΔE ≥ 15), every hue stands at 3:1 or more against the drawing's surface, and every text ink at 4.5:1 or more against the paper, the surface and the assembly bands, in both themes. Colour is never the only code: each kind of step has its own glyph (below), and its kind is written in its note and the reading.

### Focus, the masthead and the page's edges

- **Focus is one ring everywhere**: an outline of 2 px of ink, 2 px off the element (`--focus-width`, `--focus-offset`, `--focus-color`), which no other style overrides (the checked theme option shows it too) and which forced colours keep, as `Highlight`. Inside a figure the drawing rings the element itself (`.fig-focus`, `Highlight` in forced colours): a row of the journeys, a gene's bar, a step, an answer.
- **The masthead** is one bar on every page, opaque (nothing scrolled under it shows through): the brand, the navigation, then the organism (on the pages of every organism, a quiet note "All organisms" in its place, so nothing beside it moves), the load monitor and the theme, always in the same places. One row from 1081 px (the navigation never shrinks below its links; the organism's box gives way first); two rows below (the navigation across the width, its first link's text on the page's edge). On a phone two rows of 56 and 44 px (101 px in all): the brand (its mark alone below 481 px), the organism (its binomial abbreviated, `H. sapiens`, below 481 px), the load and the theme, each 44 px tall; then the six links, their text on the page's 16 px edges, scrolling sideways with a fade on the side that holds more where they do not fit (320 px), the link of the page shown brought into the row. The theme on a phone is one 44 px button showing the theme in force; it opens the three choices (a radio group, the arrow keys move the choice, a press or Escape closes it and focus returns to the button). The masthead stays in view only on screens at least 641 px wide and 600 px tall; on a phone or a short window (a zoom of 200 % and more) it scrolls away with the page, so it never covers a focused control. `--masthead-height` is its exact height at each width, and what scrolls into view (`scroll-padding-top`) stops below it where it stays in view.
- **Hover is for a mouse**: every `:hover` style is under `@media (hover: hover)`, so a tapped control returns to its resting look; on a touch screen small inline controls and the theme's options are given a finger's height.
- **Nothing widens the page.** A table scrolls inside its own frame (`.table-wrap` is the containing block of everything in it); on a phone the tables of many columns become one card per row, each cell its column's name in a column of its own and the value beside it as one run of text; panel heads wrap; echoed input wraps. The end-to-end tests check every page at 320, 768, 1024 and 2560 px (and every flow at 390 and 1440 px) for a page wider than its window and for a figure's text outside its figure or over another's (`e2e/system.spec.ts`, `e2e/helpers.ts`).

## The figure grammar

Every figure of the site (the journeys and the path up close on Convert, the answer's path view and its phone timeline, the batch's travel figure, the names' lanes, Explore's assemblies, id history, composition and matrix, the Method's stages drawn on real answers, the page of an unknown address) draws with one grammar, in `src/components/figure/` and `src/styles/figure.css`. A new figure uses it; a mark that means one thing is drawn one way everywhere, so a reader learns it once.

- **One release axis** (`releases.ts`). Every release of a graph is one column of equal width, in ascending order (a graph whose releases had a gap would still draw each release once): `releaseScale(releases, x0, x1)` in pixels for SVG, `axisOf`, `runsOf`, `bandsOf` in fractions for figures drawn in HTML; the path view's zoomable scale is adapted by `scaleOf`. `ReleaseTicks` (`axis.tsx`) draws a tick per release where there is room, longer every fifth; it labels the first and the last release, then every tenth and fifth where they touch no label already placed, every label inside the figure's own box.
- **One set of assembly bands** (`assemblyBands`, `Bands`): the runs of releases each assembly serves (`serving_assembly`), alternately shaded, a dashed hairline where the assembly changes. **No band is unlabelled or clipped**: each is named by one rule, inside the band, in the first form that fits: its name and releases, its name, its short name (the part every assembly name of the graph begins with written as its initials and "…", the rest whole, so that a short name is never a bare number: `Mf…5.0` beside `Mf…6.0` for `Macaca_fascicularis_5.0` and `_6.0`, `S…9` and `S…11.1` for `Sscrofa9` and `Sscrofa11.1`; `shortNames`); a band too narrow for any is named on a second row under the names, centred on it. The same rule names the bars of Explore's assemblies (inside the bar, else above or below it) and the bands of the HTML scales.
- **One gene bar** (`GeneBar`): a gene over the releases a path holds it, from the edge of its first release's column to the edge of its last, less a joint of 2 px where a step meets it; ink, thin (1.6 px) on a figure of many rows (the journeys), thick (8 px) on a figure of one path (the path up close, the path view); an alternative the engine considered is thin and light. Where the gene exists beyond the path, a faint dotted track continues, ending in a cap at its last release (read from its active releases, never a retirement step).
- **One glyph for each kind of step** (`glyph.ts`, `glyphs.tsx`, `glyphOf`), its hue for the family and its shape to say it again:

  | Glyph | Step | Hue |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | a tick across the bar | a new version or a replacement in Ensembl's history | history |
  | a dot | a split, a merge, or both | event |
  | an arc over the bar | a re-mapping of a gene to itself | history |
  | a diamond, filled (outlined on an alternative); a dot inside when it is also a split or merge | a history step between two assemblies (a handoff); it takes the place of the step's own glyph | handoff |
  | an open square | an identifier read through an external database | external |
  | an open square with a dot | an id a bridge passes through (two genes carry it) | external |
  | a filled square | the answer's id in the database asked (ink: the answer in Ensembl, at the release asked) | external |
  | an open circle | an Ensembl form of the identifier read as its gene, or the release the identifier is read at | ink |
  | a ring | a gene that is its own answer | ink |

  A step between releases or lanes apart is also a line in its hue (a crossing through an external database dashed); on a phone's timeline the same glyphs turn with the time axis. Legends draw the same components (`GlyphSwatch`), so a legend cannot differ from its figure.
- **One score encoding** (`ScoreTrack`): Ensembl's mapping score of each strong history step is a stem on one track under the genes' bars, from 1.0 at its top down to the score, the floor 0.9 (or the tenth under the lowest score drawn), in the hue of the step's family; a step Ensembl gave no score is an open circle on 1.0. The digits are not drawn: the exact value, as the api sent it, is on focus (the element's note) and in the reading. On a phone's timeline the track runs down beside the lanes.
- **One label placer** (`labels.ts`, `placeLabels`): every label of a figure is measured (the monospaced face by its advance, the sans by the browser), placed by one deterministic rule (the requests in priority order, each in the first of its forms and rows where it overlaps no label already placed and stays within its bounds) and left out where it fits nowhere (its full text is on focus, in the note and the text alternative). **No text collides with text, none runs out of its figure, and none is crossed by a line**: every label is drawn over the marks with a halo of the figure's ground (`.fig-text`: `paint-order: stroke`, the figure's `--fig-ground`), which cuts any line passing behind it. Only an identifier is ever shortened, cut in its middle so that its start and its version stay (`middleCut`: `ENSSSCG…08047.2`); a word, a database's name or a sentence is never cut (`wrapWords` sets it on two lines instead). On a phone's timeline labels stand in a column, pushed apart and joined to their place by a leader (`stackLabels`).
- **One note, legend and caption** (`FigureNote`, `Legend.tsx`). A figure is one tab stop: the arrow keys move along its elements in reading order, Home and End jump; pointing at an element shows the same note as focusing it. The note holds the element's exact values; it can be pointed at itself without vanishing, and Escape hides it without moving focus (`roving.ts`: `useHover`, `useDismiss`). On a touch screen a first tap shows an element and a second opens it where it opens something. Hit areas are at least 24 px. A figure is laid out for its frame's width, which one `ResizeObserver` for every figure reports after the browser's layout and before the paint (`useWidth`, taken at once with `flushSync`): no layout is read during a commit. The legend (`FigureLegend`) stands with the graphic it explains; the caption (`FigureCaption`) under it, a journal's: its letter, what is drawn, how to read it, the endpoint it was computed from.
- **Forced colours.** Every mark that carries meaning takes the system's text colour, its ground the canvas, a light element the grey text colour; the shapes keep the kinds of step apart; focus is the highlight colour (`figure.css`, `explore.css`, `home.css`).

## The path view

The path view (`src/components/PathView.tsx`) draws the api's explained path (`path.nodes`, `path.steps` and each candidate's `path` and `final_path`) and nothing else.

- **Time runs along the releases.** Left to right on a wide screen; top to bottom on a phone (below 640 px), with the same layout and encoding, the axes swapped. Behind the drawing, the assembly bands: the runs of releases each assembly serves (`serving_assembly`), named with their releases, so a handoff is seen where it happens.
- **Nodes are identifiers at releases.** An Ensembl gene is a bar over the releases the path holds it, on a lane named in the gutter by its first gene's id without version (its genes are labelled by their versions where they keep the id); a faint dotted track behind it shows the releases at which the gene exists (`active`), ending in a cap at its last release. The identifier and the forms it is read through (a transcript, a translation, an id without version) stand above the lanes, stepping down to the gene they are read as; external ids are open squares, ids a bridge passes through open squares with a dot. The answers stand in a column at the end, ranked as the api ranks them, the best filled.
- **Steps are drawn by kind**, in the figure grammar: a history step is its glyph where the old id ends and the new one begins (a tick, a dot for a split or merge, an arc for a re-mapping, a diamond for a handoff between assemblies) and, where it joins releases or lanes apart, a line in its hue from the end of one gene's bar to the start of the next. A crossing through an external database is dashed in its hue and names the database in the tooltip and the reading. Ensembl's score of each strong step is a stem on the score track under the lanes (the digits on focus and in the reading). Travel backward in time draws the same steps leftwards; the words mark such a step "walked backward".
- **The chosen path is strong, the alternatives light.** The steps and genes of the best candidates are drawn at full weight; those of every other candidate the engine reached are thinner and lighter. Pointing at a row of the candidates' table draws that candidate strongly instead; pointing at a row of the path in words rings its step in the drawing, and the step pointed at or focused in the drawing marks its row in the words (`src/lib/highlight.ts`: the views share what is pointed at without drawing each other again). The ten best-ranked candidates are drawn by default (four on a phone); the rest on request, and the page says how many are drawn.
- **A split or a merge shows that other ids took part.** The api counts the other ids of each split or merge (`splits`, `merges`) and does not name them: a short stub per id (at most three) leaves the event's glyph toward the later releases (a split) or enters it from the earlier ones (a merge), in the event's hue, and the words say how many ("the earlier id also passed to 1 other id"). "Name them", in the words, asks the api the same transition: the earlier id converted from the event's old release to its new one (a split), the later id converted back (a merge), Ensembl's history alone; the other genes of that answer are listed with their scores and the conversion that named them. No participant is drawn that the api does not send.
- **The view opens on the whole path.** Fitted to the releases of the candidates drawn (at least ten wide); where the best path holds a split, a merge or a bridge, a button of the toolbar names it ("The bridge at 78") and zooms to it (twelve releases wide). The toolbar is a group of buttons; the view it set last ("Fit the path", the event, "All releases") is pressed (`aria-pressed`, filled in ink) until a zoom or pan by hand.
- **A path at one release is no time axis.** Where the path holds no step of Ensembl's history and every step holds at one release (a symbol to its gene and on to another database), the panel is "The reading at release N": the identifier, each form or database it is read through, the gene and the answer's id, left to right (top to bottom on a phone), each node with its glyph of the grammar and each crossing named by its databases (`src/components/CrossingView.tsx`).
- **The deciding criterion in words.** Under the best answer in the answer column, "decided by" and the criterion; above the drawing, the criterion's meaning (idtrack's own text of the `Candidate` field it compares, served by the api's `/vocabulary`), the best answer's and the runner-up's values of every field its ranking compares (the score's criterion compares the steps without a score, then the score), which value ranks first, and the order in which the criteria decide. The runner-up is the first candidate that is not best, as idtrack's ranking defines it (idtrack names no runner-up in its results). The annotation, which idtrack gives as its sort key `(-external ids, -transcripts, -translations)`, is shown as the three counts by name, the sort key on focus; a tuple whose parts the api's vocabulary does not name is shown as idtrack wrote it and marked "as given".
- **Every element is focusable and exact.** The drawing is one tab stop; the arrow keys move along its elements in the order of the reading, Home and End jump, Enter pins the tooltip (`aria-pressed`), Escape closes it. Each element is named with what a reader needs first (a step: its kind, Ensembl's event in its direction, the releases, the score, an assembly handoff, the places; a gene: its kind, its life, its releases on the path, its place) and described by its note while the note is shown (`aria-describedby`). The note is never cut by the figure's frame: it stands below the element where the window has room, else above it, else on the side with more room and scrolled inside itself; it can be pointed at. In forced colours every text of the figure is the system's text colour on the canvas (`result.css`, the last block of forced colours). Hover and focus show the element's exact values as the api sent them: releases, the score (written as Python writes the float, so `0.9598796202450001` and `1.0` read as the api's text), splits and merges, databases, assemblies by name and key, the reason of the step in idtrack's words.
- **The path in words beside it** (`src/components/ResultReading.tsx`). One candidate at a time (the best first; any other chosen by its rank), its steps as an aligned table in the walk's order (the api's, but a run of re-mappings of one gene to itself listed in the walk's direction, `readingOrder`): the releases, the two ids, the kind of step, Ensembl's score (the score track's encoding turned along the line, 1.0 at the left and the path's floor at the right, and the exact value), the place, assembly or databases; a step other than Ensembl's history that starts at an earlier release than the walk stood at (a bridge that leaves a gene before the release it was read at) says "← back from release 84 to 78". On a phone each step is a card whose score has a line of its own. **Every step of Ensembl's history is written in Ensembl's direction**: the earlier id at the event's old release, then the later id at its new release, the releases ascending, whatever the walk's direction; a step the walk took from the later id to the earlier one (a conversion to an earlier release) is marked "← walked backward" apart. Two ids of one stable id are written with the shared part quiet and the version strong. A step without Ensembl's score between two versions of one stable id says that idtrack does not count it among the path's steps without a score. "Copy" writes every step of every candidate as sentences (written only when pressed); the table is the drawing's accessible description.
- **The legend keys what is drawn.** The marks the drawn candidates' paths hold (`presentMarks`), under the figure; the key to every mark of the path view on request.
- **Zoom and pan.** Drag to pan, ctrl or shift with the wheel (or a pinch) to zoom, buttons to zoom, fit the path, or show all releases. The layout is recomputed at each zoom, so labels keep their size.
- **Deterministic layout.** No force simulation: lanes are given in rank order (the best candidate's genes on the first lane, a lineage kept on one lane where its releases are free, genes alive at the same releases never on one lane), so the same answer is always drawn the same way and positions mean something (`src/lib/pathLayout.ts`). A gene's label never sits on a mark: a re-mapping's arc and a line that comes down into a lane take their extent in the label rows above the lane (`pathLabels`), and a label with no room left is left out.

### Words and values

- **Every meaning comes from the api.** The texts of statuses, reasons, step reasons, criteria, match steps and prefixes, reasons of other readings, place relations, bridge rules, candidate fields and error codes are read from `GET /api/v1/vocabulary`, which serves idtrack's own tables and the api's table of its codes; the page writes none of its own. Where idtrack gives codes without texts (its statuses) or no table (the fields a criterion compares), the api's own words stand in, marked as the api's (`source`), and the api's tests fail when idtrack's set of codes changes. A code without any text is shown as the code itself.
- **What the search weighed is shown beside the path.** Every bridge the search considered (`path.bridges`), taken or refused, with where the two genes lie relative to each other and the rule of each id they shared; the history steps it did not follow (`path.refused`), each with its rule; the other readings of the identifier (`other_readings`), with why each is reported, what it answers and at which releases; every reason of a result (`reasons`), so that an answer read at another release than the one given says so beside its targets; each gene's places (assembly, region, bases, strand, releases) and each history step's place relation on focus.
- **Values by their type.** Yes or no for a truth value; a dash for an absent value, with what its absence means on focus; numbers as the api wrote them. No value is written in a programming language's spelling; the end-to-end tests check every screen for it.
- **No answer, said plainly, and no wider than the result.** A sentence states an absence only as wide as idtrack's result does. When the best answer has no id in the database asked for (`no_target`), the headline names that database for the best answer only ("No HGNC Symbol for the best-ranked gene at release 115"), its gene is shown as what was reached, not as the answer; the other genes reached are said not to have been looked up (idtrack takes the target from the best answer only), each a link that converts it to that database, with idtrack's own sentence of its limitation where the path splits; the code stands quietly beside the status with idtrack's text on focus. Any other result without an answer says idtrack's reason in one sentence.
- **Whole values.** A table that does not fit scrolls sideways inside its frame, with a shaded edge and a line that says so; on a phone, the tables of many columns turn into one card per row, each value beside its column's name. Nothing in a cell is cut; the end-to-end tests check every screen for a value wider than its cell.
- **The end of a gene's life** is drawn from the releases at which it exists (`active`) and says so in the legend and on focus: idtrack's explained path has no retirement step (the graph's marks of a retirement are internal).

### Accuracy

The model of the view (`src/lib/pathModel.ts`) keeps every node and step object as the api sent it and only adds what drawing needs (the releases at a step's two ends, its kind, its direction, a handoff); a field the api's document does not name (an idtrack newer than the document) is kept and shown with its exact value. The unit tests check, on 124 recorded real answers with a path (of 138 conversions recorded from the pig and human graphs) (`tests/fixtures/`), which hold every reason of step but two (a final step through a transcript of another release, and a step refused between the mitochondrial genome and a nuclear sequence, which no sampled answer held; the second is checked on a recorded step standing in for one), every place relation but `no_place` (a graph made in memory), and every bridge rule but `shared_at_one_release` (a graph made in memory) and `mitochondrial_genome`: that each candidate's drawn path is its api path then final path, step for step; that the line is continuous from the identifier (an LRG record's gene id read as its gene: from that gene) to each target; that each drawn step joins its nodes at the releases the api gives; that every score shown is the api's own text (compared with the raw JSON); that the reading has every step of every candidate in the walk's order; that the deciding criterion is shown between the pair idtrack compared (equal in every field compared before it, different in it); that every bridge, refused step and other reading is listed with its values. The end-to-end tests do the same in the browser on real paths of each kind of step (`e2e/cases.spec.ts`, the `cases` of `e2e/data/<graph>.json`): a scored and an unscored version change, a replacement, a split, a merge, a handoff between assemblies, bridges with their checks and places (one from a retired gene), a split whose first-ranked gene has no id in the database asked for (the genes are shown, never another gene's id), a name read at another release, a tie, a decision by the display name, an answer in an external database.

## The answer

The answer to one conversion (`src/components/ResultView.tsx`) is a figure of lettered panels: a, the answer; b, the path through the releases; c, the path in words; d, the decision and every candidate; e, how the identifier was read; then the api's answer as sent (as sent or indented, with every value's text kept; saved as a file by "Download JSON").

- **A state mark, not a share of a line.** Panel a's top rule is the state's colour across the whole width (a plain answer's green, a tie's or a missing target's amber); a converted answer with cautions to weigh has a rule in ink and the caution's triangle beside its count.
- **The verdict first, whatever the answer's size.** The query, the arrow of releases, the answer and the status stand together at the top of panel a, top-aligned (stacked below 900 px); an identifier at display size is never broken. An answer of many targets reads as the genes they are ids of: each gene at display size with its symbol, its first six targets in small type, "and N more" with "Show all"; at most three genes at display size, then "and N more genes" (every one is in panel d). The status keeps the api's word; a converted answer with a caution to weigh is not drawn in the colour of a plain one ("with 2 cautions to weigh"). Beside it, one sentence that fits the result: where every best target is an id of one gene, that idtrack converts at the level of the gene (the answer in a database of transcripts or proteins is every id the gene carries, not the counterpart of the identifier alone); where one best answer was ranked first of several candidates, so (with Ensembl's split or merge on its path); else idtrack's meaning of the status, without the codes of fields it names (`readerText`). The deciding criterion's sentence gives the values the pair was compared on.
- **Evidence where the answer is** (`src/lib/evidence.ts`, `src/components/Evidence.tsx`). Under the status, one short mark per class of evidence a reader should weigh, read from the result's own fields: through a bridge (idtrack's inference), a bridge between two genes alive at the same release (their `active` releases meet: the gene entered did not take the place of the gene left), a bridge that chose the gene nearest in releases while another candidate reached by a bridge from the same gene carries ids the bridge taken does not (`decided_by` is `bridge_gap`: idtrack's documented class of a retired gene whose accession another gene holds), a bare number read in a database whose ids are numbers (a caution where the graph holds it only at an earlier release than the target, a note otherwise), a best answer only outside the reference sequence at the target release (`non_reference`), a best answer also listed among the copies set apart (an LRG record's gene id), every target an id of one gene (a note), a step that puts the gene elsewhere (`places_apart`), a step across assemblies whose places are not compared (`other_assemblies`), steps without Ensembl's score, a target attached at another release, read at another release than the one given, a match that is not the identifier as written (a synonym, letter case, a version newer than the graph), another meaning at other releases, another reading; and, as a note, the lowest of Ensembl's scores on the path. A caution is a triangle, a note a dot, always with words; a press opens what the result says with its values, idtrack's meaning of the code, the code itself, and the limitation of idtrack's documentation the class falls under (its lead sentence and its section in idtrack's documentation at the same documentation commit as the Method page's text: `src/generated/limitations.json`, `sections`, `scripts/limitations.ts`; a class names its section by one or more anchors, the first the generated file holds, so that a retitled section is found), and a split on the best path whose other successors were reached (a note: idtrack ranks a split's successors by Ensembl's scores, not by which lies at the old place). No threshold of the page's own: a score is stated, not judged. What the string meant at other releases is also said in a line under the marks, with "Read it at release N".
- **Symbols beside Ensembl ids** (`src/lib/symbols.ts`). Each gene the answer names (the answer, the candidates, the genes reached, the other readings' answers) carries its symbol at the target release: idtrack's own conversion of the gene at that release into the organism's name authority, asked in one batch request for the whole answer, without bridges; the authority is the first of idtrack's `SYMBOL_DATABASES` order (MGI, VGNC, HGNC) the graph can convert to at that release. A gene the authority gives no symbol shows none; where the database asked is the authority, the targets are the symbols and nothing more is asked. Panel a names the database and release the symbols are of.
- **Every identifier is a way on** (`src/components/IdMenu.tsx`). A press on an id opens a small menu: convert it from the release it holds at to the database and release asked, open it in Convert at that release to choose another database, its names at that release (an Ensembl gene) or the genes a name names (another id), Ensembl's own page of a gene's id history, and a copy.
- **Words, not codes.** Statuses, reasons, matches, step kinds, place relations and criteria are shown in words; a press opens idtrack's meaning and the code as the api writes it (`Term`, a toggletip: on a press, on focus with Enter, and on a mouse's pointing; never on hover alone). A card opened by pointing stays while the pointer crosses to it and rests on it (it closes 200 ms after the pointer has left both), Escape closes any shown card from anywhere without moving focus, and a card that would pass the window's bottom opens above its term (WCAG 1.4.13). Labels follow the result's state: "Answer", "Reached" (the genes of a result without an id in the database), "Asked for" (no answer); "Deciding criterion: none: a tie", "— (one candidate)". A decision is said once where it informs (the Ranked card and panel d).
- **An identifier that converts to nothing is given its life.** For `no_conversion`, the identifier converted at the release it is read at (with its path, without bridges) gives each gene it named there with the releases at which it exists (`active`) and its places, and `/names-of` the names the databases gave it there: each gene's releases, its assembly and its last release in words, and a bar over the releases on the axis of the graph with its last release capped, the release read and the release asked marked (`src/components/ResultLife.tsx`, at most three genes). A name authority's own id (`HGNC:11998`: a prefix that is the first word of one of the organism's symbol databases, then digits) not found is told that the graphs hold no such ids, with idtrack's limitation and the way on.
- **Where the genes lie, drawn.** Above the table of places, one row per assembly and sequence region, every row on one scale of bases (a short gene short beside a long one) and centred on its own first and last base, each distinct span a bar with its strand, drawn once for the versions that share it; the table groups the digits of bases (`55 273 409`), the exact number on hover.
- **What the search weighed, bounded.** The bridges the search considered: the taken first (the chosen path's marked and first, the versions of an id in their natural order), eight of each verdict shown and the rest on a press, the refused folded behind a count of where their genes lie, each row's shared ids four at first. An answer also listed among the copies set apart is said to be the answer itself.
- **Cite.** "Cite" opens idtrack's citation as its documentation gives it (`src/generated/idtrack_docs.json`), with the version of the idtrack that answered here, and a sentence for a methods section that states this conversion exactly: the identifier and organism, the release it was read at and how, the release and database asked, whether bridges were allowed, idtrack's and the api's versions and commit, the graph's releases and content digest, and the date (`methodsSentence`, from the answer, the graph's description and `/version`).
- **Provenance and timing.** "Provenance" saves, beside the answer as sent, which server, idtrack and graph gave it and the request that asked it (`src/lib/answerProvenance.ts`, the batch's shape). The server's time of an answer sent again from its memory (`cache;desc=hit`) or confirmed unchanged (304) says that no search ran.
- **On its way, and drawn in stages.** After two seconds the page says what it converts and for how long, with "Cancel"; a cancelled conversion is asked no more until asked again. A release in the address that is no whole number is said at once in the graph's terms ("Not a release: abc"; the releases the graph holds), and nothing is asked. An answer of 150,000 characters of text or more is drawn panel by panel, one task each (the answer and its path first), so that no long task holds the page. On a phone, an address that opens on an answer brings the answer's heading into view once.
- **Every list is bounded.** The candidates' table is paged (25 rows, 10 on a phone), with a search over target, gene and symbol; the path in words shows one candidate's steps; the answer's targets are folded beyond six. The largest real answer (368 candidates) opens with about 3,000 elements (it had 121,000).

## The batch and the names

- **A list as given, read by the form of its cells.** A pasted list or file is one identifier per line, or a table: its separator is the first of tab, comma and semicolon the text holds; a cell quoted as a spreadsheet quotes it is read without its quotes (`src/lib/batchList.ts`). Its first line is taken as a header when, in some column, nine in ten of the other lines hold numbers (or Ensembl ids) and it does not, or when its cell in the column read holds a space that no other line's does; the page says which column is read and why the first line is a header, and the person can choose the column and switch the header off. A file's encoding is read from its mark (UTF-32 before UTF-16, then UTF-8), else as Windows-1252, and the page says so, counting the identifiers that then hold letters outside plain ASCII (a sign of another encoding). Nothing here knows a gene. A not-found identifier in the form of a date a spreadsheet makes of a cell (`1-Mar`, `Sep-2`, `2023-09-02`) says so under the identifier, and such rows are counted; no symbol is guessed back.
- **Typing never waits.** The list is read a render after it is typed; a list of more than 60,000 characters only once typing pauses (300 ms), and kept for the session then too. An insertion of more than 200 lines as typed text (dictation, a drop, an automated fill) is put in by the page at the selection, since the browser's own editing takes seconds over a thousand lines; a paste stays the browser's.
- **The examples are the Convert page's sets** (`src/lib/batchExamples.ts`): the showcase's journeys grouped by their settings, each set a button that fills the list and the settings (release read at, target release, database); the empty field's placeholder is the first few of the organism's own identifiers. Under *From release*, the page says to give a dataset's annotation release, with idtrack's limitation "Without a release, a string is what it means today", and the releases each of the graph's assemblies serves.
- **Where a list comes from** (`src/components/BatchInference.tsx`, `src/lib/batchInference.ts`): *Infer it from the list* asks idtrack's source inference of the list's different identifiers (`POST …/source-inference`, mode `release`, at most `batch_max` of them) and draws it: a column per release as tall as the identifiers found there (on the assembly that serves it), the band of releases the list cannot tell apart from the best (idtrack's one-sided sign test) in ink, the best named on the axis, the margin in words; *Read the list at* fills the release, and the exports record the inference (its band, margin and request id) while the form holds that release.
- **The server's bound, and no split.** A list of more different identifiers than `batch_max` (`/limits`) is refused before anything is sent, with why the page does not split it (a batch's answers can depend on its members: an item that is another item with a separator and a number appended, `-1`, `.2`, `_3`, is read as that item's copy; and the bound is the server's) and how to convert more (idtrack's `convert_many`, or the http api in requests of the bound, each name with its copies). A list over the bound by its repeats alone is sent once per different identifier, and each answer drawn at every place of its identifier (the api answers a repeated identifier alike at each place; the set of items, and so every copy, is the same); the page and the files say so.
- **A batch is a run held outside its page** (`src/lib/batchRun.ts`): one per organism. It goes on when its page is left (an organism switch, a row's path), and the page of another organism says that it is under way or done, with a link to it. It is kept for the session once, when its answer comes (written after the answer is drawn), never on a later render. A batch is a pure function of its body, so a run that failed because nothing answered is asked again when the browser is back online or the tab is shown again. A refusal that names its wait (`Retry-After`, 120 s at most) is asked again by itself **once**, after the wait and while the tab is shown; the page says that it will be, and after a refusal of that ask, when it was asked and that nothing more is asked until the person tries again: a busy server is never asked again and again by its own page. Typing in the form draws the field alone, never the page or the results under it (the list is its own component; the results are drawn again only for a new answer).
- **One answer with every path.** A batch is asked with `explain`, so that its table, its evidence and its figure of what the paths crossed read one answer, and the api's JSON saved is that answer (2.1 MB, 96 KB compressed, for a thousand human ids). Where the server refuses the paths themselves (`response_too_large`, or a refusal naming `explain` as its argument), the batch is asked again without them, as the api advises, and the head says so. A refusal for time (`request_budget_exceeded`, `server_busy`) is no refusal of the paths: it is waited out once with them, and then *Ask without the paths* is the person's choice, which the head and the provenance record as theirs. What needs the paths is then said not to be known.
- **Drawn without holding the page.** The head comes with the answer's first frame; the rows are read from the results a slice at a time between frames (about 12 ms each) and the panels drawn in a transition.
- **The figure of a batch**, lettered, its panels' heads in the section's tier as the answer's (the letter, the serif title, a rule under both): a head that is no heading of its own (an eyebrow and one line, the count of identifiers, the different ones where some repeat, and the species in italics; the setting; the server's time with its unit and its `Server-Timing` entries on a press; the request id; a way to the downloads), brought to the top of the window when the answer comes, the focus on panel a's heading; **a, what came back**: the statuses as one thin proportional bar (10 px, a segment of a few at least 3 px) and a key that gives each status present its count in large tabular figures, its name and the api's meaning; then the counts of rows as one list of two columns (one on a tablet or phone), each its number, its name (which says, on a press, how it is counted and from which fields), a line of plain words and *Show*, a button of a fixed name that shows only its rows (`aria-pressed`). Only counts that hold rows are drawn; the counts of none are named on one quiet line ("None: answers shared or read elsewhere than 67", each name opening how it is counted), and those that cannot be counted without the paths on another. The counts: answers that need care (a caution in their evidence), other genes reached (a converted answer whose candidates below the best are other genes), another gene ranked by score alone (decided by `score`, its runner-up another gene), through a split or a merge (from the paths), through a bridge, named otherwise at the target (an identifier read as a name of a database of names, `synonym_databases`, whose answer that database names otherwise at the target: the database asked where the answers are in one, else the database beside the genes; a few pairs `SEPT9 → SEPTIN9` in its line), answers shared by different identifiers, read at a release inferred (or elsewhere than the release given), and, where there are any, identifiers in the form of a date and items read as another item's copy. **b, the results**; **c, through the releases**; **d, download and cite**.
- **One row per identifier**, in the order given, its columns of widths measured once from the whole answer (`table-layout: fixed`), so that nothing moves while a windowed table scrolls, sorts or filters: the status and, without an answer, the first sentence of idtrack's reason (a tie: equal in all the evidence, every one listed); the Ensembl gene of the best answer at the target release with its symbol beside it: by default the organism's name authority (the first of idtrack's `SYMBOL_DATABASES` the graph can convert to at that release, as the answer page's symbols), or another database the person chooses (the authority first, then the order of the target select), or none; one more batch of the genes, read and answered at the target release, nothing asked where the batch answered in that database. An identifier that converts to nothing says what it was: the last release of its gene at the release it was read at, the assembly, and the authority's name of it there (one batch per release read at, with the paths, and one into the authority: the answer page's life of a retired id, for every such row at once). Under the gene: the other genes the identifier leads to, with their symbols; the splits and merges of Ensembl's history on its best path ("split and merge at 69 → 70"); the other rows whose answer it is too, each a button (`#3`) that brings that row into view, focuses it and marks it a moment (every row shown again if a filter hid it). Then its id in the database asked; how it was read and at which release (a column left out, and said once above the table, when every row was read alike); the criterion that decided it with the two values it compared (of the first field in which the best and the runner-up differ: `0.92713 vs 0.921403` for the score), a press giving its meaning and idtrack's order of the criteria with the deciding one marked; its cautions (`components/Evidence.tsx`, `lib/evidence.ts`, from the paths too; a column left out when no row has any), the notes (exact facts that are no fault, such as the lowest of Ensembl's mapping scores on the path) in the opened row only, so that a note shown on most rows never dilutes the cautions; *Path*, for an identifier that was read: the conversion of the identifier, or, for an item read as another item's copy, of that item, whose answer the row shows. An opened row gives every mark in words (the lowest score with idtrack's definition of Ensembl's mapping score), why the best is first (the deciding criterion and its meaning, each one's values of the fields it compares, which ranks first, that the criteria before it are equal and those after it not compared), the history on the path, and every candidate with its symbol. Every list in a cell shows three ids, then how many more. The tools (filters by status, deciding criterion and kinds of step, the sort, the search) are folded behind one line, *Filter and sort*, that says when a filter or a sort is on; open for a table of more than 24 rows, folded for a shorter one and on a phone, so that the rows come first. A table that fits its frame scrolls with the page, its head held under the masthead; only a table wider than its frame scrolls in a frame of its own, both ways. On a phone each row is a card: only the card has a rule, a value that is only absent is left out, and below 400 px each value takes the card's width under its column's name, so an id stays whole on one line.
- **c, through the releases**: arcs from each release identifiers were read at to the target, each count under its arc's foot in up to three rows, a count below the first joined to its foot by a leader that passes no count of a row above (no count is set over a leader); the count read at the target itself is written at the head of the target's line, in a band no arc rises into (the caption says the counts stand under the feet only when every one found room; every count is also in the line under the axis); under them, on the same axis and bands, a column at each release where the best paths took a step of Ensembl's history (at the first release of the event's later id, in Ensembl's direction), as tall as the identifiers that stepped there, stacked in the hues of the step families, the tallest numbered with the identifiers (each once); its counts in words behind *List the steps by release*. A list read at one release draws no arc and says so in a sentence. Beside it, what the best paths crossed: each kind of step a path crossed (a new version, a replacement, a split, a merge, an assembly change, a re-mapping, a bridge, a reading through an external database) with its glyph of the grammar (a new version and a replacement share the tick, as in every figure), a bar and its count of identifiers, and a button that shows those rows; the kinds no path crossed on one quiet line.
- **Exports say where they came from** (panel d, each file a card of what it holds: the table's first rows and columns as a small table with how many more, the first characters of the api's JSON and its size in bytes, the first lines of the provenance). The api's JSON is saved byte for byte (with every path); the api's TSV (one row per candidate, no path) is a second request of the same body without `explain`, said so beside the button, its request id written into the provenance. Beside them, a table of one row per identifier made from the answer shown (`src/lib/batchExport.ts`), as TSV or CSV (RFC 4180 quoting), with `index` (the page's `#`) and `line` (the line of what was given), the person's own columns, the names beside each gene (one entry per gene, in the order of the genes), the best answer's score per gene and the runner-up with its score, the other genes reached and their names, the history on the best path (`split 69->70`), what an identifier that converts to nothing was (`last_release`, `name_at_read_release`), the evidence as this page's codes and in words with their values, `shared_with` as `#` numbers, begun by `#` lines of provenance that define every column and every evidence code it writes (the api's codes are the vocabulary's); its cells safe to open in a spreadsheet (one that begins with `=`, `+`, `-` or `@` and is no number is written after an apostrophe); and the same provenance as JSON: the request as sent and as the server resolved it (the target release, which an empty target leaves to the graph's last), every other request whose answers the table holds (the names, what retired identifiers were) with its request ids, the paths' refusal or the person's choice to ask without them, the source inference where it gave the release, the TSV's request. Under the files, the sentence for a methods section written from the run (the number of identifiers and the species, the releases, the database, idtrack's and the api's versions, the graph's digest, the bridges, the date, the statuses with their counts), with *Copy the sentence* and the way to cite idtrack itself (Method).
- **Names count genes, not versions.** Ensembl writes a gene as its id and a version, and a gene keeps its id across versions: the genes a name names are its distinct ids, each a group with its versions (in natural order), one lane per database holding every version (a tick where the version changes, the version written above its segment where the label placer finds room clear of the hairlines where the assembly changes) and each version's exact releases beside it, the first two at once and the rest on a press (`src/lib/nameGroups.ts`). The assembly bands run down the whole lane column, behind every lane, as on the scale above. Where a database's name of the gene becomes a synonym (its name lane ends the release before its synonym lane begins), a mark on the synonym's lane says from which release and what the database names the gene there: that gene version converted into the database at that release (one batch per database and release; a turn whose name cannot be had says only that the name is a synonym from then). Each gene says what the databases of its names call it at the graph's last release (one batch per database, the gene's id converted there), and the sentence over the figure names it where a database that gives the name as its name now calls the gene otherwise ("SEPT9 names one gene: ENSG00000184640 at 48–115 (named SEPTIN9 in HGNC Symbol at 115)"). The genes are paged (twelve, then more on a press, with a filter), so a name of a thousand genes stays a short page; while an answer comes, its place is held at the height of a short answer. A release of the address that is no whole number is refused on the page, as Convert refuses it, naming the graph's releases; nothing is asked. The fields' placeholders and the hint's databases are the organism's own. On a phone each gene is a card.

## Explore and Method

### Explore (`/organisms`, `/organisms/<organism>`)

- **The stories first** (`components/explore/Stories.tsx`): the place above the graphs side by side where the home's stories of Ensembl's history (the gallery of cases, the journey up close, the server's facts; `components/home/`) stand, the page's first part. Then the comparative figures of every graph.
- **Assemblies on one axis** (`components/explore/AssemblyRows.tsx`): each assembly a bar over the releases it serves, named by the grammar's rule; a name with no room inside its bar stands above it (its full, then its short form), and only where neither fits above, below it, so the rows keep one rhythm. Each change of the serving assembly a diamond in the handoff hue at its boundary, filled where Ensembl publishes a mapping of the two assemblies, open where it publishes none, dashed where idtrack's documentation says neither (`lib/explore/handoffs.ts`). Which changes are mapped is read from idtrack's own limitation "Places across an assembly change" (its two lists, `src/generated/idtrack_docs.json`), matched to the graph's assemblies by name; the organism page's table of assemblies says it in words for each change.
- **Ensembl's id history, release by release** (`components/explore/IdHistory.tsx`, from `GET /organisms/{organism}/history`): on the shared release axis over the assembly bands, the gene ids active (a line) and, per release, the history edges that end there (versions followed by another version of their id in the history hue, by a version of another id in the same hue, lighter, ids retired in ink), and below the axis the ids created there; the first release's ids are where the graph begins and are not drawn. Every release is one stop of the figure with its exact counts in words (each kind once, "N of these edges carry Ensembl's score", the versions active only where they differ from the ids), the transitions that end there, and the server's conversions whose best path passes through it (links; Enter opens the first); the same values are a list for assistive technology.
  - **Explore** draws one compact row per graph, every row on the one axis of the assemblies, drawn once under the rows; a row whose history could not be read says why in its place, with "Try again" (a graph the server does not count, 404, is left out). Two measures: counts (each row on its own scale to the next round number above its largest value, written beside the row) or shares of the gene ids active (what changed into a release over the ids active at the release before it, the ids created over those active at the release), every row on one axis of 0 to 100 %, so that organisms of different sizes compare. The legend lists only the marks drawn (no line of genes in the compact rows).
  - **An organism's page** draws it whole, each part on its own linear scale from 0 (both ends written), and the server's showcase cases as numbered marks at the release of the step that makes each its kind (`lib/explore/history.ts`, `caseMarks`: a replacement, a split, a merge, an assembly change, a bridge, a new version; the glyph of that step), keyed to the numbered list under the figure. The caption names the tallest columns, each with its count and whether the serving assembly changes there, and says which are releases within one assembly: a statement about the columns holds for every column it names.
- **The database matrix** orders its rows so that what differs stands out: the databases a conversion can end in at some releases only, by their first release, above a rule; those of every release are folded into one row ("23 databases at every release", named under the matrix), shown whole on request. Under it, **what each name holds for this organism** (`lib/databaseMeanings.ts`): idtrack's words for the name (its page "The names a graph lists": a pair "A / B" each by its own part, a database whose labels take a name of their own holding its accessions, the display names of each group with what the page says they are), the resource it comes from by idtrack's data sources (its page of authors), and the Ensembl form it is attached to (the api). The symbol databases are said for this organism from the graph's own list (`symbol_databases`, idtrack's order): the first is the organism's name authority; a later one holds what idtrack calls the symbols projected onto that organism's genes; another organism's symbol database is that organism's authority's symbols as Ensembl attaches them, not this organism's authority. A name idtrack does not describe is said to be undescribed, never explained by the page. In the stacked phone layout the names are figure text with the ground's halo.
- **An assembly's external databases** are a wrapping list of whole names (a name longer than its line wraps between its words), six at display size and the rest on request; in a card the list stands under its column's name, the card's width.
- **The composition** (`components/explore/CountsFigure.tsx`): each count of the build on a logarithmic axis beside its exact value, and under its name what idtrack says the counted things are (its concepts' page for the nodes and the two kinds of edge; the docstring of its graph's or record's member of the same name for the others; generated), or that idtrack names the count only. The rows are text, not tab stops.
- **The build** states the commit that built the graph and the version as the file records it (idtrack's statement for a graph built from a source folder, said as such), whether the benchmarks measured a graph of the same content digest (`components/method/Benchmarks.tsx`, `BenchmarkProvenance`, in plain words: the idtrack measured and the release whose answers it has, the results' commit), and how to check a graph file of one's own (`idtrack verify <graph file>` recomputes the content digest).

### Method (`/method`)

## The load monitor

The server's load (`GET /api/v1/status`, README.md, "The server's load") is shown in the masthead as a quiet reading (`src/components/LoadMonitor.tsx`): a state dot and two small readings in the monospaced face, CPU as a share of the cores available and memory as the server's footprint (PSS summed over its processes), each with a line of this session's samples. The line spans the session from its first sample (at least one closed interval, so that two samples already fill it; at most the last ten minutes), drawn 2 px inside its band so that a value of 0 or of the top, and the dot of the latest, stay inside it. Nothing blinks or moves but the numbers, which change in place at fixed widths. Below 1240 px the lines go (and a state that is not ok shows its word in place of the readings); between 1080 and 1140 px only the dot and the word stay; below 860 px the two numbers stand stacked beside the dot (down to 340 px; the dot alone below), the trigger 44 px wide and, on a phone, 44 px tall. The button's name says the state and both readings at every width ("Server load: ok. CPU 0.7 % MEM 4.95 GiB of 100.00 GiB."), and how old a reading that is not current is.

- **The state rule** (`src/lib/load.ts`): ok below 70 % of the cores available and of the memory limit; busy from 70 % of either; saturated while work waits for a worker (`requests.searches_waiting` plus `requests.connections_waiting`, the listen queue, where it was read); late while a request of `/status` has waited 4 s (`LATE_MS`, below the open interval): the values are then the last answer's; refused while `/status` answers with a refusal; unreadable while it answers with something the page cannot read (not JSON, or not of the document's form); unreachable while the page gets no answer (none within 30 s, `STATUS_TIMEOUT_MS`, is given up). A share the server could not measure (`null`, named in `unavailable`) is not used. The dot's fill says the state (the `--state-*` tokens; saturated is also a square, late a half-filled ring, refused, unreadable and unreachable a ring), and the state is written beside it when it is not ok (from 641 px), and in the button's name. A reading that is not current is drawn in the quiet ink, its lines dashed.
- **Asking.** One request at a time (a request on its way is waited for, never replaced): every 5 s while the panel is open, every 15 s while it is closed, none while the tab is hidden (`visibilitychange`); a refusal's `Retry-After` holds every ask until it has passed (opening the panel or showing the tab too).
- **The panel "Server load"** (`src/components/LoadPanel.tsx`, loaded on the first open, inside its own error boundary: if its code cannot be fetched, the reading stays and the statement stands under it, over the page, saying when the browser is offline) is a modal dialog: a popover under the masthead on a wide screen, a sheet from the bottom on a phone; focus moves into it, Escape or a click outside closes it and focus returns to the monitor. A late reading is said in a notice (since when the request waits, when the last answer came); a failure in another (refused, unreadable or no answer, the server's own words, when the page asks again). It shows, lettered as a figure's panels: a, CPU in cores used of cores available and as a share, with the source of the count (the affinity set or a cgroup quota); b, memory as PSS of the limit, RSS beside it and the limit's own use, with why PSS is the honest sum (a page the workers share with the master is counted once); c, every process (master and workers: CPU, PSS, RSS, requests with each worker's share of them, in flight, searching, waiting; the master's request cells a dash with why); on a phone each process is a card titled by its name; d, the requests answered (last 60 s, since start, 4xx, 5xx) and now (in flight, searches running, searches waiting, connections waiting in the listen queue), and the latency's percentiles over the window the api states, also on a logarithmic strip, its ticks at 1, 2 and 5 times each power of ten, labelled where a label touches no other (the powers of ten first, `placeOnLine`), the marks' names on up to three rows; then uptime, the requests lost with a worker, the graphs held, when the answer came and the age of its samples, how each number is measured (README's words), and the answer as sent.
- **Charts of the session.** The page keeps its own samples (the server reports its latest only), at most two hours of them; a failed request, a late one or a pause longer than 37.5 s is a gap in the line. The chart's cursor follows the pointer, or the arrow keys (the chart is a slider over the samples), and reads the sample in words.
- **Values.** Every number is the api's or a stated conversion of it: bytes to GiB (2^30 bytes) to two decimals, a share to a percentage to one decimal, cores to two decimals (the kernel counts CPU time in 10 ms ticks), milliseconds to one decimal, seconds in words; the api's own value is on hover, on focus and on a press of each converted value. Without an answer the reading says "unreachable" and the panel says why, never a value in place of a measurement.

## How the page behaves

- **A page's code is asked for at once.** The code of the page of the address is fetched when the app starts, beside its first requests of the server's facts (`preloadRoute` in `App.tsx`), never after them; an address that holds an identifier also fetches the answer's view beside its conversion.
- **A change of the server's graphs is seen.** A tab shown again (at most once a minute) asks `/organisms` again, revalidated against its `ETag` past the browser's cache; other content digests forget every answer held and read the organisms anew.
- **Never a white page.** Every part that can fail is inside an error boundary (`src/components/ErrorBoundary.tsx`): the root (`RootBoundary.tsx`), every page (started again at another address), the answer, the home's figures and the load monitor. A failure is said in its place, with "Try again" and "Reload the page"; the masthead and the rest stay. Code that did not arrive (the parts of the page are fetched when first needed) is asked for once more at once with its stylesheets; while the browser is online and the part is still missing, the page reloads once to fetch the site's current code and says so after the reload (`src/lib/chunks.ts`, `src/lib/lazyPart.ts`); while offline it says so and loads by itself when the connection returns.
- **Every answer is checked before a page reads it.** The client (`src/api/client.ts`) checks each JSON answer against the shape the api's document gives its endpoint (`src/api/shapes.ts`, generated; SETUP.md 14.3): an answer of another form is "an answer the page cannot read", with the place where it departs and the request id.
- **The page never asks twice.** Answers that are pure functions of the graph and the arguments (everything but `/health`, `/status` and the batch) are asked once per page session and shared: views that need one answer at once make one request, a view seen again (Back, a return to the home) is drawn from the answer held, and a request is cancelled only when no view waits for it any more. "Try again" asks again. The home converts nothing itself: its figures and cases are the server's showcase of the graph in view (`GET /organisms/{organism}/showcase`, one answer computed when the server started; `src/lib/showcase.ts`), and where the server cannot give it, the figure's place says why in the api's words, with the wait it names. A refusal that names its wait (`Retry-After`, a rate limit or a busy server) counts it down on its button and is asked again once by itself after it; a read that failed because nothing answered is asked again when the browser is back online or the tab is shown again. A second press of Convert while its conversion is on its way asks nothing more. The answers every view asks first (`/organisms`, `/version`, `/vocabulary`, `/limits`) come written into the page the server sends (a JSON block, `src/api/client.ts`, `holdPreloaded`), held from the start as if asked already, so the first paint waits for no request; an answer not of its endpoint's form is asked as any other.
- **Focus and one sentence per outcome.** A route change moves the focus to the new page's heading. A request a person makes (a submit, an example, a link of the page, Back) brings its result area into view when it starts and focuses the result's heading, or the statement of its failure, when it ends; the form is never made anew, so what was typed stays. Each outcome is said in one sentence on the page's one status line (`src/components/Announcer.tsx`): the regions that hold answers are not live regions. The tab's title names what an answer page shows (`TP53 → ENSG00000141510.20 · Convert · idtrack`).
- **What was typed survives.** An identifier, a name or a list typed and not sent, the batch's setting and its last result per organism are kept for the tab's session in the browser's session storage (`src/lib/drafts.ts`; in memory where that storage is off): across an organism switch, a row's path and Back, and a reload. A column pasted into the Convert field is offered to Batch as a list.
- **Addresses.** The page of an address is decided from its path alone (`src/lib/routes.ts`): an address that is no page is said at once; a trailing slash names the same page. A value of the address the api refuses is stated with the api's message and a way on (convert without that value). "Copy link" writes the target release and the graph's short content digest out (`to=`, `graph=` and the digest's first 12 hexadecimal digits), so that a kept link asks for the same release after the server's graphs gain newer ones, and an address whose `graph=` is not the digest of the graph the server holds says so above the answer ("This link was made on another graph"): the answer below is this graph's.
