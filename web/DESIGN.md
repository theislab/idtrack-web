# The frontend's visual direction and the path view

## The direction: an instrument

idtrack answers a precise question (what is this identifier at that release?) for people who check evidence. The page is built as a measuring instrument rather than a dashboard: calm, exact, nothing that does not inform.

- **A strict grid and hairlines.** One content width (1240 px), a 4 px spacing scale, panels separated by 1 px rules instead of shadows and cards. Controls are labelled like the panel of an instrument: a small monospaced uppercase label above the control, and the control's explanation always visible below it (never on hover alone).
- **Two faces, two jobs.** Source Sans 3 (a humanist sans) for the interface and the prose; JetBrains Mono for every identifier, release, score, digest and count, with slashed zeros and tabular figures, so that `ENSSSCG00000040723.1` reads as data and columns of numbers align. Both are served with the page (no font service).
- **One ink scale, colour only for meaning.** Text, rules and controls use a neutral ink scale on a warm paper (light) or a deep slate (dark). Hue appears only where it carries meaning: one hue per kind of step of a path, the same in the drawing, the legend, the path's reading and the empty state's figure; and a red reserved for failures. No gradients; the only motion is the loading shimmer and the zoom (both off with reduced motion).
- **Both themes are designed, not inverted.** The dark theme has its own steps of the same hues, checked separately. The theme follows the system and can be chosen (kept in the browser).

### Colour and type system

| Token | Light | Dark | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `--hue-history` | `#2f5fd0` | `#5b8def` | Ensembl's id history: a new version, a replacement, a re-mapping of a gene to itself |
| `--hue-event` | `#c34a00` | `#e0702a` | Ensembl's id history: a split, a merge, or both |
| `--hue-external` | `#00805d` | `#1f9e78` | A crossing through an external database: an identifier read as a gene, a bridge through a shared id, the id in the target database |
| `--hue-handoff` | `#c0399a` | `#d45cb8` | A handoff between genome assemblies on a step |

The four hues were chosen with a computed check, not by eye, and `tests/palette.test.ts` keeps them so: every pair stays apart under simulated protanopia and deuteranopia (Machado, Oliveira and Fernandes 2009, full severity; OKLab ΔE ≥ 8, the worst pair 8.2 in light and 8.3 in dark) and in full colour (ΔE ≥ 15), every hue stands at 3:1 or more against the drawing's surface, and every text ink at 4.5:1 or more against the paper, the surface and the assembly bands, in both themes. Colour is never the only code: history lines are solid, external crossings dashed, a split or merge carries a dot, a handoff a diamond, and each step's kind is written in its label, its tooltip and the reading.

Type sizes run 12 / 14 / 16 / 19 / 24 / 32 px; headings in the sans at weight 620 with slightly tightened tracking; labels and axis text in the mono at 10.5 to 11 px.

## The path view

The path view (`src/components/PathView.tsx`) draws the api's explained path (`path.nodes`, `path.steps` and each candidate's `path` and `final_path`) and nothing else.

- **Time runs along the releases.** Left to right on a wide screen; top to bottom on a phone (below 640 px), with the same layout and encoding, the axes swapped. Behind the drawing, the assembly bands: the runs of releases each assembly serves (`serving_assembly`), named with their releases, so a handoff is seen where it happens.
- **Nodes are identifiers at releases.** An Ensembl gene is a bar over the releases the path holds it, on a lane; a faint dotted track behind it shows the releases at which the gene exists (`active`), ending in a cap at its last release. The identifier and the forms it is read through (a transcript, a translation, an id without version) stand above the lanes, stepping down to the gene they are read as; external ids are squares, ids a bridge passes through diamonds. The answers stand in a column at the end, ranked as the api ranks them, the best filled.
- **Steps are drawn by kind.** A history step joins the release where the old id ends to the release where the new one begins; its hue says whether it is a version change or replacement, or a split or merge; a scored step shows its score above the line when it is below 1 and there is room (shortened only by cutting digits, marked with "…"; the full value is on focus). A history step between assemblies carries a diamond in the handoff hue. A crossing through an external database is dashed in its hue and names the database in the tooltip and the reading. Travel backward in time draws the same steps leftwards; the reading says "travelled backward".
- **The chosen path is strong, the alternatives light.** The steps and genes of the best candidates are drawn at full weight; those of every other candidate the engine reached are thinner and lighter. Pointing at a row of the candidates' table draws that candidate strongly instead. The ten best-ranked candidates are drawn by default (four on a phone); the rest on request, and the page says how many are drawn.
- **The deciding criterion in words.** Under the best answer in the answer column, "decided by" and the criterion; above the drawing, the criterion's meaning (idtrack's own text of the `Candidate` field it compares, served by the api's `/vocabulary`), the best answer's and the runner-up's values of every field its ranking compares (the score's criterion compares the steps without a score, then the score), which value ranks first, and the order in which the criteria decide. The runner-up is the first candidate that is not best, as idtrack's ranking defines it (idtrack names no runner-up in its results). The annotation, which idtrack gives as its sort key `(-external ids, -transcripts, -translations)`, is shown as the three counts by name, the sort key on focus; a tuple whose parts the api's vocabulary does not name is shown as idtrack wrote it and marked "as given".
- **Every element is focusable and exact.** The drawing is one tab stop; the arrow keys move along its elements in the order of the reading, Home and End jump, Enter pins the tooltip, Escape closes it. Hover and focus show the element's exact values as the api sent them: releases, the score (written as Python writes the float, so `0.9598796202450001` and `1.0` read as the api's text), splits and merges, databases, assemblies by name and key, the reason of the step in idtrack's words.
- **The reading beside it.** Every step of every candidate as a sentence, in order, with the same values; it is the drawing's accessible description and can be copied whole.
- **Zoom and pan.** Drag to pan, ctrl or shift with the wheel (or a pinch) to zoom, buttons to zoom, fit the path, or show all releases. The view opens fitted to the path's releases (at least ten wide). The layout is recomputed at each zoom, so labels keep their size.
- **Deterministic layout.** No force simulation: lanes are given in rank order (the best candidate's genes on the first lane, a lineage kept on one lane where its releases are free, genes alive at the same releases never on one lane), so the same answer is always drawn the same way and positions mean something (`src/lib/pathLayout.ts`).

### Words and values

- **Every meaning comes from the api.** The texts of statuses, reasons, step reasons, criteria, match steps and prefixes, reasons of other readings, place relations, bridge rules, candidate fields and error codes are read from `GET /api/v1/vocabulary`, which serves idtrack's own tables and the api's table of its codes; the page writes none of its own. Where idtrack gives codes without texts (its statuses) or no table (the fields a criterion compares), the api's own words stand in, marked as the api's (`source`), and the api's tests fail when idtrack's set of codes changes. A code without any text is shown as the code itself.
- **What the search weighed is shown beside the path.** Every bridge the search considered (`path.bridges`), taken or refused, with where the two genes lie relative to each other and the rule of each id they shared; the history steps it did not follow (`path.refused`), each with its rule; the other readings of the identifier (`other_readings`), with why each is reported, what it answers and at which releases; every reason of a result (`reasons`), so that an answer read at another release than the one given says so beside its targets; each gene's places (assembly, region, bases, strand, releases) and each history step's place relation on focus.
- **Values by their type.** Yes or no for a truth value; a dash for an absent value, with what its absence means on focus; numbers as the api wrote them. No value is written in a programming language's spelling; the end-to-end tests check every screen for it.
- **No answer, said plainly, and no wider than the result.** A sentence states an absence only as wide as idtrack's result does. When the best answer has no id in the database asked for (`no_target`), the headline names that database for the best answer only ("No HGNC Symbol for the best-ranked gene at release 115"), its gene is shown as what was reached, not as the answer; the other genes reached are said not to have been looked up (idtrack takes the target from the best answer only), each a link that converts it to that database, with idtrack's own sentence of its limitation where the path splits; the code stands quietly beside the status with idtrack's text on focus. Any other result without an answer says idtrack's reason in one sentence.
- **Whole values.** A table that does not fit scrolls sideways inside its frame, with a shaded edge and a line that says so; on a phone, the tables of many columns turn into one card per row, each value beside its column's name. Nothing in a cell is cut; the end-to-end tests check every screen for a value wider than its cell.
- **The end of a gene's life** is drawn from the releases at which it exists (`active`) and says so in the legend and on focus: idtrack's explained path has no retirement step (the graph's marks of a retirement are internal).

### Accuracy

The model of the view (`src/lib/pathModel.ts`) keeps every node and step object as the api sent it and only adds what drawing needs (the releases at a step's two ends, its kind, its direction, a handoff); a field the api's document does not name (an idtrack newer than the document) is kept and shown with its exact value. The unit tests check, on 120 recorded real answers with a path (of 130 conversions recorded from the pig and human graphs) (`tests/fixtures/`), which hold every reason of step but two (a final step through a transcript of another release, and a step refused between the mitochondrial genome and a nuclear sequence, which no sampled answer held; the second is checked on a recorded step standing in for one), every place relation but `no_place` (a graph made in memory), and every bridge rule but `shared_at_one_release` (a graph made in memory) and `mitochondrial_genome`: that each candidate's drawn path is its api path then final path, step for step; that the line is continuous from the identifier to each target; that each drawn step joins its nodes at the releases the api gives; that every score shown is the api's own text (compared with the raw JSON); that the reading has every step of every candidate in order; that the deciding criterion is shown between the pair idtrack compared (equal in every field compared before it, different in it); that every bridge, refused step and other reading is listed with its values. The end-to-end tests do the same in the browser on real paths of each kind of step (`e2e/cases.spec.ts`, the `cases` of `e2e/data/<graph>.json`): a scored and an unscored version change, a replacement, a split, a merge, a handoff between assemblies, bridges with their checks and places (one from a retired gene), a split whose first-ranked gene has no id in the database asked for (the genes are shown, never another gene's id), a name read at another release, a tie, a decision by the display name, an answer in an external database.
